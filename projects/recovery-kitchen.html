RECOVERY KITCHEN / A learning game, one meal at a time.
How the scoring works & sources

This educational rubric is not a validated nutrition or health assessment. It starts with recovery coverage, then subtracts composition deductions. Food data are representative recipe estimates, including the saturated-fat and sugar values, not verified product labels. Sources below explain nutrition concepts; they do not certify the dataset or the score.

Recovery coverage awards up to 40 points for protein, 40 for carbohydrate, 10 for produce variety and 10 for drinks. Coverage is capped at 100%, cubed, multiplied by the category maximum, then rounded down. Protein or carbohydrate below 50%, 75% or 90% of its benchmark caps the base score at 39, 59 or 79 respectively. Drinks below 250 mL cap it at 69; 250–499 mL at 89. An incomplete produce challenge caps it at 94. The lowest ceiling applies.

Composition deductions use estimated whole-meal calories (4 × protein grams + 4 × carbohydrate grams + 9 × fat grams). Saturated-fat calorie share = 9 × saturated-fat grams / calories. Added-sugar share = 4 × added-sugar grams / calories. Deep-fried share uses all calories from foods modeled as deep-fried.

  • Saturated fat: no deduction at or below 10% of calories; rises linearly to 25 points at 20% or more.
  • Added sugar: no deduction at or below 5% of calories; rises linearly to 25 points at 20% or more. The 5% start is a deliberately strict game choice.
  • Deep-fried reliance: 20 × min(1, deep-fried calorie share / 40%), up to 20 points. Nuggets, fries, glazed donuts and the modeled pre-fried instant ramen are flagged. Other cooking styles are not automatically flagged.

Deductions are rounded to the nearest whole point, then subtracted from the capped recovery score. The displayed score is at least 1. Preparation and nutrient deductions can overlap by design. Percentages change when the meal composition changes; this rubric cannot judge whether the resulting total portion is appropriate.

WHO saturated-fat guidance and FDA-described added-sugar guidance address the whole daily diet, not a mandatory post-exercise meal limit. Our meal thresholds, weights and preparation deduction are game conventions. Naturally occurring sugars in whole fruit and plain dairy are excluded from added sugar. Honey and syrups are counted as added sugar. 100% juice sugars are not added sugars, but do count as free sugars under WHO’s broader definition; this game does not fully assess free sugar intake.

Total fat is displayed, while saturated fat affects the grade. Calories per gram of protein are displayed for comparison, not scored: needed carbohydrates and unsaturated fats can increase that ratio. Lower calories or less total fat are not automatically better recovery nutrition.

Daily protein reference = body weight in kilograms × 0.8. The approximate equivalent is 0.36 g/lb; for unit consistency the game always calculates protein from kilograms using 0.8. Daily carbohydrate reference = body weight in pounds × 2.5 for practice or lifting (the moderate-exercise scenario), or × 4.0 for a game (the intense-training scenario). Pounds are calculated as kilograms / 0.45359237. Calculations retain full precision; display values are rounded to one decimal place.

The meal protein and carbohydrate benchmarks are each one-third of the respective daily reference, assuming an equal three-meal allocation. This split is a game-design assumption, not a research-derived post-exercise target. Daily reference totals are shown separately from meal benchmarks; the game does not ask a player to eat a whole day’s intake in one meal.

The requested protein formula is the general healthy-adult RDA, not an athlete-specific recommendation. NIH sports-nutrition guidance describes higher protein intakes for athletes (commonly 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day) and distinguishes adult and adolescent requirements. Training duration, age, growth and individual needs are not modeled by these selected formulas. A high game score does not establish adequate daily nutrition.

Produce coverage is the smaller of total produce servings / 2 and qualifying foods / 2. Each qualifying food must contribute at least half a produce serving. Two servings across two foods earn full points. This variety challenge and the 500 mL beverage goal are game conventions, not clinical recovery requirements. Food moisture is not counted as a drink.

Height stays in the athlete profile, but does not independently change recovery targets. Height and weight alone cannot establish calorie needs, body composition or sweat losses. We do not calculate BMI, calorie limits or weight-loss targets. Young athletes also have growth needs that this game does not model. Scores assess a simulated meal, never a person’s body. Sodium, fiber, trans fat, micronutrient adequacy and total daily energy needs are not graded.

Browse protein, carbs, vegetables and fruit, fats and extras, or drinks. Treats and fast foods are mixed into these groups. Mixed foods may appear in more than one group; category placement does not affect scoring. Macro labels and live totals stay hidden until grading. Each food card includes optional vitamin and mineral highlights, also shown after grading. These qualitative notes do not earn points, remove deductions, or establish that a portion meets daily needs. All foods share the same transparent scoring rules.

Meals can include desserts and convenience foods. We explain their contributions and gaps rather than assigning a blanket “bad food” label. A single meal cannot measure daily nutrition, hydration status, or health. Young athletes have growth needs that this game does not model.

Micronutrient roles follow NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets, including vitamin C, vitamin K, calcium and potassium. Food notes describe typical ingredients, not tested products, guaranteed amounts or performance boosts.

Nutrition values are rounded, representative educational estimates per listed serving, not laboratory measurements or exact product-label data. Calories shown use the simplified 4 kcal/g protein + 4 kcal/g carbohydrate + 9 kcal/g fat calculation; actual labels may differ.

Guidance: Australian Institute of Sport: protein · Sports Dietitians Australia: recovery nutrition. Additional references: WHO: healthy diet and fat quality · FDA: added versus total sugars. These sources inform concepts, not the numerical game formula or the estimated food values. For personal fueling needs, work with a qualified sports dietitian.
